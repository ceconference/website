2022 edition

The New Age of Liberalism

The traditional liberal and conservative divide is experiencing tumultuous changes in reaction to unprecedented global events. This year's Central European Conference will aim to answer what role key public agents, such as political actors, enterprises, media and international organisations play in this systemic transformation. We will focus on the practical implications of liberalism as a political and economic ideology in a series of panels, debates and workshops.

Organised by the University of London Czech & Slovak Societies together with the LSE Hungarian and KCL Polish societies, with the support of the LSE Annual Fund.

Keynote speakers

Keynote

Věra Jourová

Hailing from the Czech town of Třebíč, Věra Jourová has been the European Commission‘s Vice-President for Values and Transparency since 2019. As part of her role, she has been tasked with overseeing the maintenance of rule of law, and ensuring that the European Union’s democratic values and freedoms are upheld. Before this, she held the post of the Minister of Regional Development, before going on to become the European Commissioner for Justice, Consumers, and Gender Equality. In 2019, Forbes Magazine named her as one of their Top 100 most influential people of the year. She has previously voiced concerns over the status of rule of law in Poland.

Keynote

Jacques Rupnik

Meet Jacques Rupnik, the french political scientist, and one of our Keynote Speakers! Born in Czechoslovakia, he completed his Undergraduate studies and his PhD at the Sorbonne, finishing his Master’s degree at Harvard in between. Much of his research is dedicated to the European integration and democratisation processes of Central and Eastern Europe. Amongst his most acclaimed books are The Other Europe, as well as 1989 as a Political World Event. In 1988, he participated in an interview for a six-hour documentary called The Other Europe. He has worked as an advisor to the former Czech president, Václav Havel, and currently serves as a research professor at the Centre d’Etudes et de Recherches Internationales (CERI).

Programme

The conference was built on three pillars – panels, roundtable discussions and workshops. Each offered a distinct topic and way for participants to engage, tackling issues facing Central Europe from social, economic, political and cultural angles.

Panels

A panel brings together a small number of speakers who investigate a specific issue. Each of them is given time to present their view, followed by an in-depth discussion. The panel also engages with the audience, taking in questions directed either at the panel as a whole, or at specific speakers.

EU and the Visegrad Four

V4 versus EU: what next?

Jiří Přibáň, Milada Anna Vachudová, Karolina Zbytniewska, Michal Šimečka

Media Accountability

How and to what extent can we hold the media accountable? Where should the responsibility for informing the public lie?

Zuzana Kovačič Hanzelová, Karel Kovář, Anna Gielewska, Ágnes Urbán

Roundtables

A roundtable is a structured discussion through which participants are confronted with a specific issue. It often takes the form of a loose debate, with each speaker given equal time to present their solutions and deliberations regarding the issue.

Ryszard Petru, Michael Žantovský, Krisztián Orbán, Jakub Kratochvíl

Research & Development

Radek Špicar, Martin Mudrak, Gábor Takács-Carvalho, Jakub Dovčík
